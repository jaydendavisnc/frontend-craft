# Intent

Read this before anything else in the skill. A beautiful page that ignores what the person asked for is a failure, however good it looks. Most bad outcomes start here: the model hears "landing page", stops listening, and builds its favourite page.

## Who decides

In this order, and the higher one always wins:

1. **What the person said.** Their exact words, their colours, their fonts, the sections they asked for, the references they named.
2. **What they already have.** An existing brand, codebase, design system, logo or product. Extend it; never redesign it because you were asked for one screen.
3. **What their words imply.** "For my dental clinic" implies calm, trust, real prices and a booking path, even if they never said so.
4. **The dealt hand.** It fills only what the first three leave open. It is a push away from your habits, not a set of orders.
5. **The defaults in this skill.** The floor, used when nothing above says otherwise.

When the hand or a default disagrees with the person, the person wins, and you say in your closing note what you set aside. Never argue someone out of what they asked for, and never quietly replace it with what you think is better. If you believe another direction would serve them better, build what they asked for, build it well, and offer the alternative in one line at the end.

## Read a brief in four layers

Write these down, in a few words each, before you choose anything:

1. **The literal ask.** What artefact, for whom, with what content and what hard constraints: "a landing page for my AI notes app, dark, with pricing".
2. **The named things.** Every site, brand, era, style, colour, font, section or effect they mention. Each one is an instruction. List them, and do not lose any.
3. **The mood words.** Clean, premium, fun, bold, minimal, modern. These are instructions too, but they need translating. See the table below.
4. **The unsaid.** Who will see it, on what device, what they must do there, and what would embarrass the owner. A clinic's page that looks like a nightclub has failed, even if nobody said "not a nightclub".

Then write one sentence: "They want X, for Y, that feels like Z." If you cannot write it, read the brief again before you build.

## What people mean

Mood words are compressed instructions. Decode them into decisions, and avoid the literal reading, which is nearly always the slop version.

| They say | They mean | They do not mean |
|---|---|---|
| Clean | Control. One idea per screen, nothing misaligned, nothing without a job | White space and hairlines around tiny grey text |
| Modern | Current product craft: precise type, real product, considered motion, nothing dated | Gradients, glass and glowing blobs |
| Minimal | Few elements, each one excellent, with strong scale contrast | Empty, timid, or a page with nothing on it |
| Premium, luxury, high-end | Restraint, real materials and photography, generous scale, slow confident motion, perfect details | Gold, black and serif italics everywhere |
| Professional | Trustworthy, clear and finished, with real information first | Boring, corporate stock photos, blue |
| Bold | One or two moments at a scale that feels reckless, a confident colour, a strong idea | Everything loud at once |
| Fun, playful | Personality in motion and copy, things that respond to you, surprise that rewards | Clip-art, emoji, rainbow colours, bouncing everything |
| Sleek, slick | Smooth, fast transitions, tight type, nothing clunky | Dark mode with neon |
| Techy, futuristic | Precision, real data and interface, engineered motion | Matrix code, grids of glowing lines, sci-fi fonts |
| Warm, friendly, cosy | Real people and places, soft natural light, a human voice, a warm palette chosen as a set | Beige everything, hand-drawn fonts, cartoon mascots |
| Editorial, magazine | Typography leads, real photography, a grid you can see, long-form rhythm | Italic serif headlines on cream as a costume |
| Brutalist, raw | Honest structure, system fonts or mono, visible grid, hard edges, used with craft | Broken layouts and unreadable text |
| Make it pop | One loud moment: scale, colour or motion at a single point | Brighter everything |
| Wow, stunning, award-winning | A signature idea nobody has seen for this subject, executed perfectly, with real content inside | Effects for their own sake |
| Simple | Easy to understand at a glance | Plain or unfinished |
| Not generic, not template | Built from this subject's own objects, with nothing from a kit | Weird for the sake of it |
| Like Apple | The product huge and perfectly lit, few words, cinematic scroll, perfect type | A white page with a phone in the middle |
| 3D | Real depth that belongs to the subject: a product, a place, an object you can turn | A spinning abstract blob |
| Animated | A score: an opening, motion that explains, one big moment, responsive hovers | Everything fading up |

## Named references

When someone names a site, brand or style, they are pointing at one or two qualities, not asking for a copy. Work out which qualities, and carry those.

- **Framer:** true black, tight grotesk type, product shown large, smooth scroll-linked motion.
- **Linear:** dark precision, dense real interface, one accent, engineered calm.
- **Stripe:** precise light pages, one authored colour field, product fragments at real density.
- **Apple:** the product as the hero, huge and lit, few words, cinematic pinned scroll.
- **Vercel:** monochrome, grid discipline, developer-honest copy.
- **Notion:** friendly line illustration, warm neutrals, plain words.
- **Awwwards sites:** a signature interaction, art-directed imagery, typographic boldness, transitions with no seams.
- **Chess.com, Duolingo, any consumer app:** the job of that product, its audience and its energy. Invent your own brand: never its name, logo, colours or copy.

If a reference is unknown to you, say so in one line, and build from what the rest of the brief says.

## Reading feedback

Feedback is a symptom. Translate it into the cause, then fix the cause everywhere it appears, not just in the place they pointed at.

| They say | Usually means | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Boring, mid | No loud moment, no real content, every scene the same volume | Add one signature idea and one scene at reckless scale, and put real things in the frames |
| Generic, template, AI slop | Kit components, stock moves, the probable layout | Rebuild from this subject's own objects. Run `slop.mjs`, and sweep every sibling of the pattern they named |
| Cheap | Default fonts, drift in spacing and radii, small details unfinished, weak pictures | Tighten the system, upgrade the type, fix every hover and edge |
| Too much, cluttered | Several ideas per screen, decoration without jobs | Cut. One idea per screen. Read `clarity.md` |
| Too empty | Scale is polite, nothing carries the space | Make one thing much bigger, or bring a picture in |
| Too dark, too blue, too purple | A colour habit | Change the ground, choose colour from the subject |
| Feels off | Alignment and spacing drift | Put everything on one grid and one spacing scale |
| I love it, but X | The direction is right | Change X and nothing else |

When someone flags one instance of a pattern, assume they will find every other instance next. Fix all of them in the same pass.

## When to ask

Almost never, for a website or a design. Decide, build and state your assumptions at the end, because a finished thing to react to is worth more than a question.

Ask first only when a wrong guess is expensive and a guess is unavoidable: their real prices, their real legal claims, which of their existing brand assets to use, or spending money on images. Ask in one short question, with your proposed answer, so they can just say yes.

## Ambition is not disobedience

Being ambitious means going further inside what they asked for, not swapping it for something else. If they ask for a dark minimal page, make the most surprising dark minimal page they have seen, with one signature idea, real content and perfect craft. Do not make it light because a light page was dealt.

The ambition lives in the idea, the composition, the motion and the components. The constraints come from the person.
