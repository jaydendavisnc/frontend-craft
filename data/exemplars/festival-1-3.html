The 2027 lineup

Most festivals make you choose between stages. We book 27 acts, not 80, so that you can see all of them.

Most festivals make you choose between stages. We book 27 acts, not 80, so that you can see all of them.

Friday August 20

  • The Bar. Lubec, ME. Eighteen steel pans on a flatbed. It is the first thing you hear as you walk across.

  • The Slip. New Bedford, MA. Cape Verdean morna with a six-piece band, from the town that has been singing it longest in this country.

  • The Net Loft. Moncton, NB. Acadian fiddle. She is 74, plays seated and tells long stories between reels.

  • The Slip. Portland, ME. A noise-rock trio we have asked back three times. Earplugs are free at the token booth.

  • The Packing Floor. Worcester, MA. Eleven-piece highlife band. Their last set here ran 20 minutes over and we let it.

  • The Slip. Burlington, VT. Two modular synths and a pedal steel. The sun goes down at 7:39, about two-thirds of the way in.

  • The Net Loft. Dearborn, MI. Oud and tape delay, in a room built to dry nets.

  • The Packing Floor. Montréal, QC. Ninety minutes and a full string section, the longest set she has played in the US.

  • The Bar. Machias, ME. Vinyl only. Plays as the road dries out and the day tickets walk home.

Saturday August 21

  • The Bar. Lewiston, ME. A gospel quartet that started in a church basement in 1988, singing unaccompanied.

  • The Slip. Floyd, VA. Old-time string band from the Blue Ridge: twin fiddles, clawhammer banjo and a washtub bass.

  • The Net Loft. Chicago, IL. Piano and bass clarinet. The piano came over the bar on a hay wagon.

  • The Packing Floor. Detroit, MI. Motorik four-piece who tend to play one song for most of the hour.

  • The Slip. Minneapolis, MN. Dance band with two drummers, in the best slot we have: the hour before sunset.

  • The Net Loft. Providence, RI. Kora and electric bass, and the quietest set of the weekend.

  • The Slip. Halifax, NS. Dub from a city with the same tide as ours. The sound system travels in a horse trailer.

  • The Packing Floor. Brooklyn, NY. Seven-piece art-rock band playing their first show on an island, and their first without a click track.

  • The Bar. Vinalhaven, ME. Three lobstermen and one very large speaker, playing rocksteady from a collection of 45s.

Sunday August 22

  • The Bar. Biddeford, ME. A Franco-American kitchen party, moved outdoors and onto a truck.

  • The Slip. Hilo, HI. Slack-key guitar, a father and daughter, on their first trip to the East Coast.

  • The Net Loft. Buffalo, NY. Forty singers in a 220-seat loft, with no microphones.

  • The Slip. Houston, TX. Alt-R&B with a horn section she borrowed from her church.

  • The Packing Floor. Fort Kent, ME. Country songs in two languages, on her first trip home in nine years.

  • The Net Loft. Albuquerque, NM. Solo classical guitar, unamplified, in the quietest room on the island.

  • The Slip. San Antonio, TX. Three sisters playing conjunto as fast as punk.

  • The Packing Floor. Memphis, TN. She is 71 and asked for the 11 pm slot, so she has it.

  • The Bar. Hadlock Island, ME. The people who built the festival, on the flatbed, until the truck has to go.
