Numbers from the ferry ticketing job, March 2026: 14.2 TB, Postgres 12 to 17.

Every move runs through the same five stages

  1. Audit

    2 days, €3,400 fixed

    I read pg_stat_statements, your replication setup and your last three incident reports. You get a written plan and a price, and you can take that plan to someone else.

  2. Copy

    11 days

    Schema first, then each table, then logical replication. The old primary keeps serving everything while the new one catches up.

    184 ms lag at peak hour

  3. Shadow

    3 weeks

    Real read traffic is mirrored to the new primary and every result is compared. All 41 mismatches on this job came from one collation change.

    1.92 bn rows compared

  4. Cut over

    A Tuesday, 07:00

    PgBouncer holds connections while the last WAL lands and I promote the new primary. Queries wait a few seconds and none of them fail.

    4.1 s write pause

  5. Hold

    14 days

    Replication runs backwards, new to old, so you can return with one command. Then I shut the old primary down and hand your team the runbook.
